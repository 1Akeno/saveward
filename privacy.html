Privacy policy

Last updated 17 November 2026. This policy covers the SaveVault desktop app for Windows.

Summary

SaveVault runs on your PC. It has no servers and no accounts of its own, and it doesn't collect analytics. Its developer never receives your files or your data.

Data on your PC

SaveVault reads your game save files and copies them into backup files on your PC. Settings and backups are stored in your Windows user folder (%APPDATA%\SaveVault) and stay there until you delete them.

Google Drive

If you choose to connect Google Drive, SaveVault asks Google for the drive.file permission. This only lets SaveVault create files and read back the files it created, in a folder called SaveVault. It can't see, read or change any other file in your Drive.

  • What's uploaded: your game save backups (zip files), and only to your own Google Drive.
  • Why: so you can restore your saves later or on another PC.
  • Where your sign-in is kept: a sign-in token is stored in Windows Credential Manager on your PC. It isn't sent to the developer or anyone else.
  • Sharing: SaveVault doesn't share, sell or transfer your Google data to anyone, and doesn't use it for advertising or to train AI models.
  • Removing access: disconnect in SaveVault, or remove SaveVault at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Delete the SaveVault folder in your Drive to remove the uploaded backups.

SaveVault's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Other connections

  • Save database: SaveVault downloads a public list of game save locations from GitHub about once a week.
  • Cover art (optional, can be turned off): game pictures are loaded from Steam's image servers. That request tells Steam which game's picture is being shown.
